NORTHSTAR FACILITY OPERATIONS MANUAL

Document ID: NS-OPS-2026-01
Revision: 1.4
Facility: Northstar Controlled Agriculture Research Facility
Location: Bellweather Research Campus
Effective Date: 12 March 2026

1. INTRODUCTION

The Northstar Controlled Agriculture Research Facility is an experimental indoor hydroponics facility designed to study automated crop production under tightly controlled environmental conditions. The facility contains four primary cultivation zones, a nutrient preparation room, a water-treatment section, an environmental control system, and a centralized monitoring platform known as ORBIT.

Northstar is used primarily for research on lettuce, basil, dwarf tomatoes, and experimental leafy crops. It is not a commercial food-production facility. Crop yield is important, but experimental repeatability, environmental stability, and complete data collection have higher priority than maximum production.

This manual describes normal facility operation, environmental limits, nutrient preparation, alarm handling, equipment responsibilities, maintenance intervals, and emergency procedures.

Unless a procedure explicitly states otherwise, operators must record manual interventions in ORBIT.

2. FACILITY LAYOUT

Northstar contains four cultivation zones:

Zone A: Leafy Greens
Zone B: Herbs
Zone C: Fruiting Crops
Zone D: Experimental Crops

Each cultivation zone has an independent nutrient loop and independent lighting controls. Zones A and B share one air-handling unit, designated AHU-1. Zones C and D share AHU-2.

The nutrient loops are designated NL-A, NL-B, NL-C, and NL-D.

All four loops receive purified water from the central reverse-osmosis system. However, nutrients are mixed independently for each zone. Water from one cultivation zone must never be intentionally transferred to another zone.

The nutrient preparation room contains two mixing tanks:

Mixing Tank M1 has a nominal capacity of 1,000 liters.
Mixing Tank M2 has a nominal capacity of 600 liters.

M1 is normally used for Zones A, B, and C. M2 is normally reserved for Zone D and for small experimental batches.

Neither mixing tank directly supplies plants. Nutrient solution is prepared in a mixing tank, verified by an operator, and then transferred into the appropriate zone reservoir.

The facility's primary purified-water storage tank is designated PW-1 and has a usable capacity of 4,500 liters.

3. ORBIT MONITORING SYSTEM

ORBIT is the central monitoring, logging, and alarm system used throughout Northstar.

ORBIT records:

air temperature;
relative humidity;
carbon dioxide concentration;
nutrient solution temperature;
nutrient solution pH;
electrical conductivity;
reservoir level;
pump state;
lighting state;
water-treatment status;
door status for controlled rooms;
and selected energy-consumption measurements.

Most sensors report to ORBIT once every 30 seconds.

Nutrient pH and electrical conductivity sensors are sampled every 10 seconds because rapid changes in these values can indicate dosing or mixing problems.

ORBIT retains high-resolution sensor records for 90 days. After 90 days, the data is compressed into five-minute averages. These five-minute averages are retained for five years.

Operators should not assume that absence of an alarm means that every system is functioning correctly. Certain failures, such as a partially obstructed irrigation emitter, may affect only a small number of plants and may not produce a facility-level alarm.

4. OPERATOR ROLES

Northstar recognizes three operational roles.

Level 1 Operators may perform routine inspections, acknowledge informational alarms, measure reservoirs manually, and carry out approved cleaning tasks.

Level 2 Operators may perform all Level 1 tasks and may also prepare nutrient solutions, restart irrigation pumps, calibrate approved sensors, and respond to standard environmental alarms.

Level 3 Operators may modify control parameters, authorize operation outside normal experimental limits, perform emergency isolation procedures, and approve equipment return to service following major faults.

A Level 1 Operator may not independently change nutrient dosing parameters.

A Level 2 Operator may change nutrient dosing parameters only within the range defined in an approved experiment protocol.

Changes outside an approved experiment protocol require Level 3 authorization.

5. ENVIRONMENTAL CONDITIONS

5.1 Zone A: Leafy Greens

Zone A normally contains lettuce and similar leafy crops.

Daytime air temperature target: 21.0 degrees Celsius.
Nighttime air temperature target: 18.0 degrees Celsius.

The normal acceptable daytime range is 20.0 to 22.0 degrees Celsius.
The normal acceptable nighttime range is 17.0 to 19.0 degrees Celsius.

Relative humidity target: 62 percent.

Relative humidity between 55 and 70 percent is considered normal.

Zone A lighting operates for 16 hours per day under the standard production program.

The standard light period begins at 05:00 and ends at 21:00.

5.2 Zone B: Herbs

Zone B normally contains basil, mint, and other herbs.

Daytime temperature target: 23.0 degrees Celsius.
Nighttime temperature target: 19.0 degrees Celsius.

Relative humidity target: 60 percent.

The standard lighting period is 18 hours. Lights switch on at 04:00 and switch off at 22:00.

Basil trials may use modified lighting schedules if specified in the experiment protocol.

5.3 Zone C: Fruiting Crops

Zone C is used primarily for dwarf tomato and pepper experiments.

Daytime temperature target: 24.0 degrees Celsius.
Nighttime temperature target: 19.0 degrees Celsius.

Relative humidity target: 58 percent.

Standard lighting duration is 16 hours, from 06:00 until 22:00.

Zone C is the only normal cultivation zone in which supplemental carbon dioxide enrichment is used.

During the light period, Zone C has a carbon dioxide target of 850 parts per million.

Carbon dioxide injection must stop if the measured concentration exceeds 1,100 parts per million.

If Zone C exceeds 1,300 parts per million, ORBIT generates a high-priority alarm and commands the carbon dioxide supply valve closed.

5.4 Zone D: Experimental Crops

Zone D does not have a permanent environmental setpoint.

Its temperature, humidity, lighting, and nutrient targets are defined by the currently active experiment protocol.

If no experiment is active, Zone D should be maintained in standby conditions:

air temperature: 20 degrees Celsius;
relative humidity: 55 percent;
lights off;
irrigation disabled except for maintenance testing.

6. NUTRIENT SOLUTION PARAMETERS

Electrical conductivity, abbreviated EC, is used as an operational indicator of total dissolved ionic nutrients.

The standard EC targets are:

Zone A: 1.7 mS/cm
Zone B: 1.9 mS/cm
Zone C: 2.4 mS/cm

Zone D has no universal EC target. Its target is defined by experiment.

The standard pH target for Zones A, B, and C is 5.8.

A pH between 5.6 and 6.1 is considered the normal operating range for those three zones.

A pH below 5.3 or above 6.4 requires operator intervention.

The nutrient system uses phosphoric-acid solution to reduce pH and diluted potassium-hydroxide solution to increase pH.

Operators must never add concentrated pH-adjustment chemicals directly to a cultivation reservoir.

All manual pH corrections must be diluted according to the chemical handling procedure and added through the designated dosing port while the circulation pump is operating.

7. NUTRIENT PREPARATION

Before preparing nutrient solution, an operator must verify:

the intended cultivation zone;
the required final volume;
the current experiment protocol;
the nutrient formulation identifier;
and the cleanliness status of the selected mixing tank.

Standard nutrient preparation follows this sequence:

First, fill the mixing tank with purified water to approximately 80 percent of the required final volume.

Second, start the mixing agitator.

Third, add Nutrient Concentrate A.

Fourth, allow at least two minutes of mixing.

Fifth, add Nutrient Concentrate B.

Sixth, allow at least five minutes of mixing.

Seventh, add purified water until the required final volume is reached.

Eighth, measure EC.

Ninth, measure pH.

Tenth, adjust pH if necessary.

Eleventh, allow the solution to mix for at least three additional minutes.

Twelfth, perform a final EC and pH measurement.

Nutrient Concentrate A and Nutrient Concentrate B must never be combined in concentrated form.

Combining the concentrates before sufficient dilution can cause precipitation and may permanently alter nutrient availability.

If precipitation is visibly observed in a mixing tank, the batch must not be transferred to a cultivation reservoir. The batch must be marked as rejected and handled according to the nutrient-disposal procedure.

8. RESERVOIRS AND IRRIGATION

Each cultivation zone has one primary nutrient reservoir.

Zone A reservoir capacity: 1,200 liters.
Zone B reservoir capacity: 900 liters.
Zone C reservoir capacity: 1,500 liters.
Zone D reservoir capacity: 700 liters.

Normal operation keeps each reservoir between 35 percent and 85 percent of usable capacity.

A low-level warning is generated at 30 percent.

A critical low-level alarm is generated at 20 percent.

At 15 percent, the corresponding primary irrigation pump is automatically stopped to reduce the risk of dry running.

The irrigation pumps are designated P-A1, P-B1, P-C1, and P-D1.

Each zone also has a standby pump, designated with the number 2. For example, P-A2 is the standby irrigation pump for Zone A.

Standby pumps do not start automatically during ordinary operation.

Automatic standby-pump start is permitted only when the primary pump reports a motor fault and reservoir level is above 25 percent.

If the primary pump stops because of low reservoir level, the standby pump must not start.

9. WATER TREATMENT

Incoming municipal water passes through three main treatment stages before entering PW-1.

Stage 1 is sediment filtration.

Stage 2 is activated-carbon filtration.

Stage 3 is reverse osmosis.

The reverse-osmosis unit is designated RO-1.

Under normal conditions, RO-1 produces between 380 and 430 liters of purified water per hour.

A permeate production rate below 320 liters per hour for more than 20 continuous minutes generates a maintenance warning.

A production rate below 250 liters per hour generates a high-priority fault.

The conductivity of purified water leaving RO-1 should normally remain below 40 microsiemens per centimeter.

If purified-water conductivity exceeds 70 microsiemens per centimeter for more than five minutes, water is automatically diverted away from PW-1.

The diverted water is sent to the non-process drain and must not be used for nutrient preparation.

10. LIGHTING SYSTEM

All cultivation lighting uses dimmable LED fixtures.

Lighting intensity is specified using photosynthetic photon flux density, or PPFD.

Typical standard PPFD targets are:

Zone A: 220 micromoles per square meter per second.
Zone B: 280 micromoles per square meter per second.
Zone C: 360 micromoles per square meter per second.

Zone D follows the active experiment protocol.

Lighting power can be adjusted between 10 percent and 100 percent.

Values below 10 percent are not permitted during normal cultivation because fixture output becomes insufficiently uniform.

Following a power outage longer than five minutes, cultivation lights do not immediately return to full intensity.

Instead, ORBIT applies a ten-minute ramp from 20 percent output to the scheduled output level.

This ramp reduces electrical inrush and limits sudden light exposure to the plants.

11. AIR HANDLING

AHU-1 serves Zones A and B.

AHU-2 serves Zones C and D.

The systems share air-handling equipment, but airflow to individual zones is independently controlled using motorized dampers.

A failure of AHU-1 therefore potentially affects both Zone A and Zone B.

A failure of AHU-2 potentially affects both Zone C and Zone D.

If an air-handling unit completely stops, ORBIT commands the affected zones' supplemental lighting to 50 percent intensity after five minutes.

If air handling remains unavailable for 20 minutes, lighting is reduced to 25 percent.

If air handling remains unavailable for 45 minutes, the cultivation lights in the affected zones are switched off.

This staged lighting reduction is intended to limit heat accumulation.

Irrigation continues unless another condition requires it to stop.

12. ALARM PRIORITIES

ORBIT alarms are divided into four priority classes.

Priority 0: Informational.

These events require no immediate response. Examples include completion of an automated cleaning cycle or an operator login.

Priority 1: Advisory.

These conditions should be reviewed during the current operator shift but normally do not threaten crops or equipment immediately.

Priority 2: High Priority.

These alarms require prompt operator investigation.

Priority 3: Critical.

These alarms indicate conditions that could rapidly damage equipment, invalidate an experiment, or create a safety hazard.

Priority 3 alarms generate an audible facility alarm and send notifications to the Level 3 on-call operator.

An operator may acknowledge an alarm without resolving it. Acknowledgment only records that the alarm has been seen.

The alarm remains active until the underlying condition returns to an acceptable state or an authorized operator manually clears it after inspection.

13. SENSOR CALIBRATION

pH sensors are calibrated every seven days.

EC sensors are calibrated every 14 days.

Carbon dioxide sensors are checked every 30 days and fully calibrated every 90 days.

Air-temperature and relative-humidity sensors undergo verification every 60 days.

Reservoir level sensors are function-tested every 30 days.

The standard pH calibration uses pH 4.00 and pH 7.00 reference solutions.

A pH sensor must be replaced if it fails calibration twice consecutively after cleaning.

An EC sensor does not automatically require replacement after one failed calibration. It should first be cleaned, inspected for deposits, and recalibrated.

14. CLEANING AND SANITATION

Cultivation reservoirs are cleaned between crop cycles.

Routine cleaning uses a facility-approved peroxide-based cleaning solution.

After cleaning, reservoirs must be rinsed until the measured conductivity of the final rinse water is less than 20 microsiemens per centimeter above the conductivity of the incoming purified water.

Mixing tanks M1 and M2 must be rinsed after every nutrient batch.

A full sanitation cycle is required if:

visible biological growth is found;
a rejected batch contained precipitation;
a reservoir has remained stagnant for more than 72 hours;
or contamination is suspected.

Tools used in Zone D may not be used in Zones A, B, or C without undergoing full sanitation.

This requirement exists because Zone D frequently contains experimental organisms, unconventional nutrient formulations, or microbial treatments.

15. POWER FAILURE BEHAVIOR

Northstar has an automatic backup generator.

Following loss of utility power, the generator normally begins supplying emergency circuits within 12 seconds.

Not all facility equipment is connected to emergency power.

Emergency-powered systems include:

ORBIT servers;
network equipment;
essential sensors;
primary circulation pumps;
water-treatment controls;
emergency lighting;
and ventilation controls.

Cultivation LED lighting is not normally powered by the emergency generator.

If utility power returns within five minutes, lighting resumes according to the ten-minute ramp procedure.

If the outage lasts more than five minutes, lighting schedules resume from the current clock time rather than attempting to compensate for all missed lighting hours.

Operators must not extend the current light period automatically to replace lost light unless an experiment protocol specifically requires compensation.

16. TEMPERATURE EXCURSIONS

An environmental temperature excursion begins when zone temperature remains outside its normal operating range for more than ten consecutive minutes.

A brief reading outside the range does not automatically count as an excursion.

If temperature exceeds the normal range but remains less than 3 degrees Celsius beyond the applicable limit, a Priority 1 advisory is generated after ten minutes.

If temperature reaches 3 degrees Celsius or more beyond the applicable normal limit, a Priority 2 alarm is generated.

For Zone A during the daytime, for example, the normal upper limit is 22 degrees Celsius. A temperature of 24 degrees is above the normal range but remains less than 3 degrees above the upper limit. A temperature of 25 degrees reaches 3 degrees above the upper limit and therefore qualifies for the higher alarm condition if sustained according to the applicable alarm logic.

A temperature above 30 degrees Celsius in any active cultivation zone generates an immediate Priority 3 alarm.

17. NUTRIENT TEMPERATURE

Nutrient solution temperature is controlled separately from air temperature.

For Zones A, B, and C, the standard nutrient-solution target is 19 degrees Celsius.

The normal range is 17 to 21 degrees Celsius.

If nutrient temperature exceeds 23 degrees Celsius for more than 15 minutes, ORBIT generates a Priority 2 alarm.

If nutrient temperature exceeds 26 degrees Celsius, a Priority 3 alarm is generated immediately.

Zone D nutrient temperature is experiment-specific, but unless explicitly overridden, the same safety limits of 23 and 26 degrees Celsius apply.

18. WATER LEAK DETECTION

Leak sensors are installed under each reservoir, beneath both mixing tanks, and near RO-1.

Detection of water by a single leak sensor generates a Priority 2 alarm.

If water is simultaneously detected by two or more leak sensors in the same equipment area, ORBIT escalates the event to Priority 3.

A leak alarm does not automatically shut down the entire facility.

Instead, ORBIT attempts to isolate the associated equipment.

For example, a leak beneath the Zone B reservoir causes the Zone B refill valve to close and prevents automatic transfer of additional nutrient solution into that reservoir.

Irrigation does not automatically stop unless reservoir level becomes unsafe or an operator determines that continued circulation is increasing the leak.

19. DAILY OPERATOR INSPECTION

At the beginning of each staffed day shift, an operator performs a physical inspection.

The inspection includes:

checking all four cultivation zones for visible plant stress;
checking floors for leaks;
checking irrigation emitters for obvious blockages;
confirming reservoir levels;
checking mixing-tank cleanliness;
reviewing unresolved ORBIT alarms;
checking RO-1 operating status;
and confirming that emergency exits are unobstructed.

The physical inspection is required even when all ORBIT indicators show normal conditions.

The inspection result is recorded as one daily facility inspection entry, with separate comments for any abnormalities.

20. IRRIGATION EMITTER CHECKS

Automated pressure measurements can identify major irrigation failures, but they cannot reliably detect every partially blocked emitter.

For this reason, irrigation distribution is manually sampled once per week.

For each active zone, an operator selects ten emitters distributed across the cultivation area.

Each selected emitter is operated for exactly 60 seconds, and the discharged volume is measured.

An emitter is considered within tolerance if its measured flow is within plus or minus 8 percent of the nominal emitter flow.

If two or more of the ten sampled emitters are outside tolerance, an expanded inspection of at least 30 emitters must be performed.

If more than 10 percent of the expanded sample is outside tolerance, the irrigation line must be cleaned before the next standard irrigation cycle.

21. DATA QUALITY AND EXPERIMENT VALIDITY

Northstar research places strong emphasis on traceability.

A crop experiment is not automatically invalid merely because an alarm occurred.

Instead, alarms and environmental excursions are included in the experiment record and reviewed by the responsible researcher.

However, some events require automatic experiment-quality flags.

An experiment receives a "Major Environmental Deviation" flag if:

air temperature remains outside the normal experimental range for more than 120 cumulative minutes during any 24-hour period;
relative humidity remains outside the allowed experimental range for more than 180 cumulative minutes during any 24-hour period;
or carbon dioxide in a carbon-dioxide-controlled experiment remains outside the experimental range for more than 90 cumulative minutes during the light period.

A flag indicates that the data requires review. It does not automatically mean that the experiment must be discarded.

22. MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE

The following intervals apply under normal operating conditions.

pH sensor calibration: every 7 days.
EC sensor calibration: every 14 days.
Reservoir level-sensor test: every 30 days.
Carbon dioxide sensor functional check: every 30 days.
Carbon dioxide sensor full calibration: every 90 days.
Temperature and humidity sensor verification: every 60 days.
RO-1 prefilter inspection: every 30 days.
RO-1 membrane performance test: every 180 days.
Backup generator functional test: every 30 days.
Emergency-stop functional test: every 90 days.
Mixing-tank agitator inspection: every 60 days.
Primary and standby irrigation-pump inspection: every 90 days.

Maintenance intervals may be shortened if equipment performance deteriorates.

They may not be extended beyond the listed intervals without Level 3 authorization and a documented engineering justification.

23. EQUIPMENT IDENTIFICATION

Northstar equipment identifiers follow a consistent naming scheme.

P-A1 means the primary irrigation pump for Zone A.
P-A2 means the standby irrigation pump for Zone A.

The same pattern applies to Zones B, C, and D.

NL-A means the nutrient loop for Zone A.

AHU-1 serves Zones A and B.
AHU-2 serves Zones C and D.

M1 is the 1,000-liter primary mixing tank.
M2 is the 600-liter secondary mixing tank.

PW-1 is the 4,500-liter purified-water storage tank.

RO-1 is the reverse-osmosis unit.

CO2-C1 is the primary carbon dioxide control valve for Zone C.

24. EMERGENCY STOP SYSTEM

Emergency-stop buttons are located:

outside the nutrient preparation room;
at the main cultivation corridor entrance;
and beside the electrical control room.

Activating an emergency stop removes power from major process actuators, including nutrient-transfer pumps, irrigation pumps, chemical dosing pumps, and carbon dioxide injection.

ORBIT servers and emergency monitoring remain powered.

Emergency ventilation also remains available.

Emergency-stop activation does not disconnect the entire building electrical supply.

After an emergency stop has been activated, the system cannot be returned to normal operation merely by releasing the button.

A Level 3 Operator must inspect the affected systems and authorize reset.

25. CHEMICAL DOSING FAILURE

Automated nutrient and pH dosing is controlled by ORBIT through dedicated dosing pumps.

If a dosing pump is commanded on continuously for longer than 90 seconds, ORBIT stops that dosing pump and generates a Priority 2 alarm.

This limit is intended to reduce the risk of uncontrolled chemical addition.

A second dosing attempt is not automatically initiated.

An operator must inspect the dosing line, pump, chemical level, and sensor reading before manually authorizing another dosing operation.

If the event involves pH-adjustment chemical, a manual pH measurement using an independent handheld meter is required before dosing resumes.

26. COMMUNICATION FAILURE

Individual controllers continue basic local operation if communication with ORBIT is lost.

For example, an irrigation controller may continue its previously loaded schedule even while the central server cannot communicate with it.

A controller communication loss lasting more than 60 seconds creates a Priority 1 advisory.

After five minutes, it becomes a Priority 2 alarm.

If communication remains unavailable for 30 minutes, an operator must physically inspect the affected zone.

Loss of ORBIT communication therefore does not necessarily mean irrigation or environmental control has stopped.

27. MANUAL OVERRIDES

Manual overrides should be used only when required for maintenance, emergency response, or an approved experiment.

Every manual override must have:

the operator identity;
the time it was enabled;
the affected device or parameter;
the reason;
and, when known, the intended end time.

ORBIT generates a reminder if a manual override remains active for more than two hours.

After six hours, an active override generates a Priority 2 alarm unless the experiment protocol explicitly permits a longer override.

Overrides affecting safety interlocks require Level 3 authorization regardless of duration.

28. SAMPLE INCIDENT

At 14:12 on a hypothetical operating day, Zone B reservoir level decreases to 29 percent.

ORBIT generates a low-level warning because the reservoir is below the 30 percent warning threshold.

The irrigation pump continues operating.

At 14:19, reservoir level falls to 19 percent.

A critical low-level alarm is generated because the level is below 20 percent.

The pump still remains capable of running because the automatic stop threshold is lower.

At 14:23, the level reaches 15 percent.

ORBIT stops P-B1.

P-B2 does not automatically start because the primary pump was stopped due to low reservoir level rather than a motor fault.

This distinction is intentional. Starting the standby pump would not correct the lack of nutrient solution and could cause the standby pump to run dry as well.

29. SAMPLE AIR-HANDLING INCIDENT

At 10:00, AHU-2 stops because of a motor-protection fault.

Because AHU-2 serves both Zones C and D, both zones are affected.

At 10:05, ORBIT reduces the supplemental cultivation lighting in Zones C and D to 50 percent.

At 10:20, because AHU-2 is still unavailable, lighting is reduced to 25 percent.

At 10:45, lighting is switched off.

Irrigation continues because no irrigation-related fault exists.

If AHU-2 returns to service at 10:50, lighting does not necessarily jump immediately to 100 percent. ORBIT returns lighting according to the applicable recovery controls and active experiment settings.

30. SAMPLE WATER-QUALITY INCIDENT

At 07:30, RO-1 permeate conductivity rises from 32 to 76 microsiemens per centimeter.

No diversion occurs immediately because the high-conductivity condition has not persisted for five minutes.

At 07:33, conductivity remains at 75 microsiemens per centimeter.

Water still has not yet been automatically diverted because only three minutes have elapsed.

At 07:35, the conductivity remains above 70 microsiemens per centimeter.

The five-minute threshold has now been reached, and ORBIT diverts the produced water away from PW-1.

The diverted water is discharged to the non-process drain.

Previously stored water already present in PW-1 is not automatically discarded solely because new RO water failed the conductivity limit.

31. PRIORITIES DURING MULTIPLE FAULTS

When several problems occur simultaneously, personnel safety takes priority over crop protection.

Equipment protection is second.

Prevention of cross-contamination is third.

Preservation of experimental conditions is fourth.

Crop preservation is fifth.

Data completeness is important but must never take priority over personnel safety or safe equipment operation.

For example, an operator must not enter an area with a suspected electrical hazard merely to prevent loss of an experimental crop.

32. FINAL OPERATING PRINCIPLES

Northstar is designed around four operating principles.

First, the evaluator of system state should rely on both automated monitoring and physical inspection.

Second, experimental reproducibility requires that interventions and abnormal conditions be recorded.

Third, automated protection systems should prevent a single fault from escalating when practical.

Fourth, operators should not bypass safety or contamination controls merely to preserve crop yield.

When this manual conflicts with a currently approved experiment protocol, the experiment protocol may override normal cultivation parameters such as temperature, humidity, lighting, irrigation timing, nutrient EC, or carbon dioxide targets.

An experiment protocol may not override facility-wide safety limits unless the exception has been separately approved by a Level 3 Operator and documented as an engineering deviation.
